
# Aaron Vogel | CS 499 ePortfolio

Welcome to my Computer Science capstone ePortfolio. This ePortfolio contains my professional self assessment, my code reviews, my original and enhanced artifacts and the narratives that go with the work and the skills that I have developed.

## Professional Self-Assessment

My time in construction and in healthcare taught me how to deal with the real world and take ownership of the outcomes. In construction you need the right materials and the right plans if you're going to get anything done. In the operating theatre you need accurate information and reliable processes if you're going to look after the patients. That's the kind of experience I've had and that's the kind of approach I bring to computer science. I want to build software that is useful, that is reliable and that is clear to the people who are using it.

Both of those disciplines taught me how to work with people in different roles and with different levels of knowledge. On a construction job you've got a plan that has to make sense to the customer, the supervisor and a number of trades. In the OR you've got surgeons, nurses and all sorts of other people who have to share information and make decisions together. I learned how to listen, how to explain what I see and how to speak up when something could impact the next step. In CS we applied that in our code reviews where we'd explain the code and the design decisions we were making and use the feedback from the instructor to improve our work. We also learned that comments and documentation should provide other people with enough context to understand what the code is doing.

My course work has definitely improved my ability to take a problem and break it down into steps, then select an appropriate solution. In the machine learning module I used the Poker Hand dataset and discovered that linear regression was a terrible fit for the task of predicting the categories of hands. That was a useful lesson in that a program can run and still be using the wrong approach. You have to consider the problem, pick the right method and then assess the results. You also have to be aware of the trade-offs involved (e.g. speed, memory usage, how difficult it will be to maintain the solution etc).

The software development, testing, databases and secure coding courses helped me to build up a much more robust development process. I learned to break down the work into stages, test the changes as I went and then iterate the software as issues were found. My CS 499 journals looked at AI coding agents, post-quantum crypto, cloud computing and APIs. That gave me a good grounding in how the technology landscape is evolving and how the decisions we make around software can have an impact on people and organisations. I now pay much more attention to whether the information is accurate, whether access is appropriate and whether we're protecting sensitive data.

I want to keep developing my skills in software development, testing, databases and security. My experience in construction and healthcare gives me a very practical view of the problems that software has to solve. My CS course work has given me the technical skills to be able to contribute to those solutions.

How My Artifacts Fit Together

Travlr Getaways is a clear demonstration of my software design and engineering skills. I've separated out the auth, validation and database concerns, added JWT protection and improved error handling. I've also written targeted backend tests. This work has really helped me to understand the value of good organisation and good testing in terms of making an application easier to maintain and safer to use.

LumberTrack shows off my work with algorithms and data structures. The ArrayList is used for the inventory display, the HashMap for lookup by item ID and the PriorityQueue for the low stock items. There are tests for lookup, priority ordering, updates and sync after a deletion. In the portfolio I'll go into the trade-offs (the cost of removing an existing item from the PriorityQueue as part of an update being one of them).

Grazioso Salvare demonstrates my database skills. I've added MongoDB validation, indexing, pagination, aggregation and safer data operations. The benchmark script shows how I intended to test the performance but the actual gains from indexing will need to be measured against a real database. The two projects together illustrate how software design, algorithms and databases all work together to support the user and solve real problems.
